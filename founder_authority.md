# Owner Authority and Decision-Making Scope

**Document Type:** Internal Governance and Public Transparency Disclosure
**Applies To:** Decision-making authority exercised in Vipto's name

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the scope, source, and limits of the authority exercised by Vipto's Owner in operating Vipto. It exists to prevent ambiguity as to who may make binding decisions in Vipto's name, and to make clear that this authority, while real and operationally significant, does not arise from or constitute a statutory corporate office.

## 2. Scope

This document applies to all decisions made in connection with Vipto's product direction, organizational structure, internship program, public representation, and day-to-day operations. It does not apply to decisions delegated to other functional roles, which are addressed separately in `authorized_representatives.md` and `roles.md`.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | The individual, Ayush Vaidya, who holds ultimate responsibility for Vipto's operations and decisions. |
| **Operational Authority** | The practical ability to direct Vipto's activities, distinct from any statutory corporate authority such as that of a company director. |
| **Delegated Authority** | Authority exercised by another individual (for example, an Engineering Lead) on the basis of the Owner's decision to assign that responsibility, revocable at the Owner's discretion. |

## 4. Identity of the Owner

Vipto's Owner is **Ayush Vaidya**. This is stated once, clearly, and factually, as the basis for the authority structure described in this document. No other individual associated with Vipto is identified by name in this or any other Vipto document; all other functions are described by role only.

## 5. Source of the Owner's Authority

Because Vipto is not incorporated, as established in `legal_entity_status.md`, the Owner's authority does not derive from any statutory office such as a company directorship under the Companies Act, 2013, or a designated partner position under the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008. Instead, the Owner's authority derives from the simple and lawful fact that the Owner is the individual who conceived of, operates, and directs Vipto as an unincorporated initiative. This is a factual and practical source of authority, recognized under general principles of Indian contract and civil law, rather than a status conferred by a specific incorporation statute.

## 6. Scope of the Owner's Authority

The Owner holds authority over the following matters:

1. **Product Direction** — determining what Vipto's product will become, including feature prioritization and roadmap decisions.
2. **Organizational Structure** — determining how Vipto's internal functions are organized, including the creation, modification, or removal of functional roles.
3. **Engagement Decisions** — determining whether and how interns and contributors are engaged, subject to the terms described in `internship.md` and related documents.
4. **Public Representation** — approving how Vipto is described publicly, including in documentation, marketing material, and communications such as this repository.
5. **Documentation Approval** — approving the content and issuance of organizational policy documents, including this one.
6. **Delegation** — assigning specific operational authority to other individuals, such as an Engineering Lead, for defined functional areas, as described in `authorized_representatives.md`.

## 7. What the Owner's Authority Does Not Include

To avoid any overstatement, the Owner's authority explicitly does **not** include or create the following, regardless of how it is exercised:

1. It does not constitute a statutory directorship, managing directorship, or company officer position, because no such positions exist in the absence of incorporation.
2. It does not itself create a separate legal entity, and exercising this authority does not incorporate Vipto by implication.
3. It does not exempt the Owner from personal legal responsibility for actions taken in Vipto's name, as explained further in `legal_entity_status.md`.
4. It does not, by itself, create statutory employer obligations toward interns or contributors beyond what is actually agreed in each individual's specific engagement terms.
5. It does not confer regulatory licenses, tax registrations, or compliance certifications that have not been separately and actually obtained.

## 8. Delegation of Authority

The Owner may delegate specific, defined areas of operational authority to other individuals occupying functional roles within Vipto, such as an Engineering Lead responsible for technical direction and intern supervision. Delegated authority is:

- Limited to the specific functional area for which it is granted;
- Exercised in Vipto's name but subject to the Owner's overall responsibility;
- Revocable by the Owner at any time; and
- Not, by itself, evidence of a statutory corporate office held by the delegate.

Further detail on delegated authority is provided in `authorized_representatives.md` and `roles.md`.

## 9. Accountability

Because Vipto is unincorporated, the Owner bears personal accountability for decisions made in Vipto's name, in the manner described in `legal_entity_status.md`. This accountability is not diminished by the existence of functional roles or delegated authority; delegation distributes operational responsibility but does not transfer ultimate legal accountability away from the Owner in the absence of a separate incorporated entity.

## 10. Responsibilities

The Owner is responsible for ensuring that all authority described in this document is exercised consistently with applicable law, that delegated authority is clearly communicated to relevant individuals, and that this document is updated promptly if Vipto's structure changes, including in the event of future incorporation.

## 11. Limitations of This Document

This document describes the operational and organizational authority currently exercised within Vipto. It is not a constitutional document of an incorporated entity, such as Articles of Association, and should not be treated as equivalent to one. Where a specific transaction or relationship requires clarity on legal authority beyond what is described here — for example, signing a significant commercial contract — that matter should be assessed independently, as described in `contracting_authority.md`.

# Intellectual Property Ownership

**Document Type:** Legal and Organizational Disclosure
**Applies To:** Ownership of source code, designs, documentation, and other intellectual property created in connection with Vipto

## 1. Purpose

This document sets out, in formal terms, how intellectual property created in connection with Vipto is treated, who is intended to hold ownership of it, and what legal frameworks are relevant to that ownership. It supplements the general contribution principles described in `intellectual-property.md` with a more formal legal treatment.

## 2. Scope

This document applies to source code, user interface and user experience designs, product documentation, technical documentation, and other creative or technical work produced by the Owner, interns, or contributors specifically in connection with Vipto's product development.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Work** | Any copyrightable or otherwise protectable output created in connection with Vipto, including code, designs, and documentation. |
| **Contributor** | Any individual, including interns, who creates a Work in connection with Vipto. |
| **Assignment** | A transfer of ownership of intellectual property from one party to another, which under Indian law generally requires a written and signed instrument for copyright. |
| **License** | Permission to use intellectual property without a transfer of ownership. |

## 4. Applicable Legal Frameworks

Intellectual property created in connection with Vipto is potentially governed by several Indian statutes, applied according to the nature of the work in question:

- **The Copyright Act, 1957**, governing ownership and protection of original literary works (including source code, under Section 2(o), which defines "literary work" to include computer programs), artistic works (including UI/UX designs), and related rights.
- **The Trade Marks Act, 1999**, governing protection of brand names, logos, and other distinctive marks, to the extent any such registration is pursued.
- **The Patents Act, 1970**, which may be relevant only in the rare circumstance that a genuinely novel technical invention, as opposed to ordinary software implementation, is developed, noting that software patentability under Indian law is narrow and subject to specific statutory exclusions.
- **The Indian Contract Act, 1872**, governing the validity of any assignment or licensing agreement entered into regarding intellectual property.

This document does not assert that Vipto currently holds any registered trademark, patent, or other registered intellectual property right; no such registration is claimed.

## 5. Default Ownership Position Under Copyright Law

Under the Copyright Act, 1957, the author of a work is generally its first owner, subject to important exceptions. Where a work is created by an employee in the course of their employment under a contract of service, the employer is generally the first owner under Section 17 of the Act. Where a work is created by an independent contributor who is not an employee, ownership generally remains with that contributor unless it is validly assigned.

Because Vipto's interns and contributors are not, in the ordinary case, represented as employees under a formal contract of service, as clarified in `roles.md` and `internship.md`, the statutory employer-ownership exception under Section 17 may not automatically apply to their contributions. This makes clear, written assignment or licensing terms important, rather than assumed.

## 6. Vipto's Intended Ownership Arrangement

Consistent with Vipto's onboarding and engagement practice, it is Vipto's intention and expectation that:

1. Source code, designs, and documentation created by an intern or contributor **specifically for, and in the course of, their engagement with Vipto**, are intended to be owned by, or irrevocably licensed to, the Owner for Vipto's use, on the basis of the terms accepted by that individual at the time of engagement.
2. This intention is given legal effect through the written internship or contribution terms provided at onboarding, which constitute the assignment or license instrument for the purposes of the Copyright Act, 1957, to the extent such terms are actually agreed and, where required for assignment, executed in writing and signed.
3. In the absence of a specific written assignment for a particular contribution, this document alone does not itself effect a legal assignment; it records Vipto's intended position, and the actual, enforceable ownership terms depend on the specific agreement made with the individual contributor.

## 7. Pre-Existing Intellectual Property

Nothing in this document, or in any other Vipto document, transfers ownership of intellectual property that a contributor or intern created prior to, and independently of, their engagement with Vipto. A contributor introducing pre-existing proprietary work into a Vipto repository should disclose this and clarify its treatment before doing so, as described in `intellectual-property.md`.

## 8. Third-Party and Open-Source Material

Intellectual property owned by third parties, including open-source libraries incorporated into Vipto's codebase, remains subject to its own license terms and is not owned by Vipto merely by virtue of use, as described further in `open-source.md`.

## 9. Ownership of the Brand

Ownership of the "Vipto" name and any associated logo is addressed in `ownership.md`. This document is limited to intellectual property created in the course of product development, such as code and design assets, rather than brand identifiers.

## 10. What This Document Does Not Do

This document does not itself constitute a signed assignment of copyright for the purposes of Indian law, and does not create new intellectual property rights. It records Vipto's position and intention regarding ownership, which takes legal effect through the specific written terms agreed with each individual contributor, as described in Section 6.

## 11. Responsibilities

The Owner is responsible for ensuring that intern and contributor onboarding materials include clear, written terms addressing intellectual property ownership, consistent with this document. Contributors and interns are responsible for disclosing any pre-existing intellectual property they introduce and for not incorporating third-party material without appropriate rights to do so.

## 12. Limitations of This Document

This document provides a general framework and does not resolve ownership of any specific piece of work in the absence of the underlying written agreement with the relevant contributor. Any dispute regarding ownership of a specific contribution should be resolved by reference to that individual's actual engagement terms, and, where necessary, with independent legal advice.
